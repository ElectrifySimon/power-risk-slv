The country

A small country on the front line

El Salvador is the smallest and most densely populated country in Central America. It sits in the Central American Dry Corridor and in the path of tropical storms.

Hurricane Mitch in 1998, Tropical Storm Agatha in 2010 and Tropical Storm Amanda in 2020 all caused flooding and landslides that damaged infrastructure and cut power.

Floods

Heavier rain, mostly in the east

Maximum five-day rainfall, the indicator used for flood threat, rises over the century. It rises most in the eastern and central regions.

Droughts

Longer dry spells in the south and east

The number of consecutive dry days increases, especially in the south and east. This affects water for hydropower, cooling at thermal plants and the crops that feed biomass plants.

Heatwaves

Very hot days everywhere

The number of very hot days rises sharply across the whole country, most in the south. Average temperatures could rise by 1.5–2.5 °C by mid-century.

Method

From hazard maps to asset risk

IRENA’s Climate Risk Assessment Tool combines the climate projections with the location of every asset, and with how sensitive each asset type is and how well it can adapt. Every asset gets a risk score for each hazard and period: low, moderate, high or critical.

Substations · near term

More than half of substations already at high flood risk

Of the country’s 159 substations, 89 (56%) face high or critical flood risk in the near term, 16 of them critical.

Substations · mid-century

Four in five by mid-century

By 2041–2070 this rises to 128 substations (81%), with 47 at critical risk. For transmission substations, the backbone of the grid, it reaches 90%.

Heat

Every substation feels the heat

Heat puts transformers under stress. In the near term, 14 substations face high heatwave risk. By mid-century, all 159 do.

Hydropower

Hydropower depends on rain

10 of the 19 hydropower plants face high or critical drought risk in both periods. Most are smaller plants.

The large reservoirs on the Lempa river rely on sub-basins that extend into Guatemala and Honduras. Planning for them has to look beyond the border.

Solar PV

Solar panels lose efficiency in the heat

The country has 1 941 solar PV installations, most of them small distributed systems. Today, 14% face moderate heatwave risk.

By mid-century, almost all of them do, and the panels themselves lose output as temperatures climb.

Transmission

Towers follow the same curve

Transmission towers at high or critical flood risk rise from 7% to 32%, and 978 of the 3 026 towers reach that level by 2041–2070.

Risk can be reduced

Raised equipment, flood barriers, dry cooling and dynamic line rating all move the score. The dashboard holds every number behind this story, the measures by asset and hazard, and the full register.
