Power under pressure

El Salvador’s grid runs through a landscape of volcanoes, river valleys and a narrow coastal plain. Floods, droughts and heatwaves reach all three, and the exposure grows with every decade.

Now

More than half of the substations are already exposed.

Under the near-term climate, 89 of 159 substations sit at high or critical flood risk. Extreme rainfall concentrates in the east and the centre, where the Lempa and its tributaries carry the water down.

By mid-century

Four in five, if nothing changes.

The share rises to 81% in 2041–2070. Transmission towers follow the same curve, from 7% to 32%, and the towers that carry the lines south of the capital are the most exposed.

Heat

Every substation feels the heat.

Very hot days increase across the whole country. By mid-century all 159 substations reach high or critical heatwave risk, against 9% in the near term. Transformers lose capacity exactly when demand for cooling peaks.

Generation

Generation carries the same exposure.

1 813 of 1 999 generation assets reach high or critical flood risk by 2041–2070, most of them solar. Hydropower faces the drought side: 10 of 19 plants at high or critical risk, on reservoirs fed by rivers shared with Guatemala and Honduras.
